Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What Poway Homeowners Should Know

Drywall acts like a wick. Paper and gypsum pull water up from the floor line, so the wet zone frequently ends a foot or more past the highest point the water itself touched. From the room, the surface may seem dry and sound even though the hidden face and the insulation are saturated. For that reason our team relies on moisture meter readings rather than appearance.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. Walls that are mushy, falling apart, moldy or contaminated get a flood cut instead, where our crew cuts straight across above the damage and takes out whatever sits under that line.

Signs to Look For

  • Baseboards that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Door casing and trim joints separating from the drywall
  • Staining in a horizontal band just above the baseboard
  • Bubbling, wrinkled or peeling paint and wallpaper low on the wall
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • One wall noticeably cooler under your hand than the rest
The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

The bottom edge of the drywall takes in water from the floor and it begins moving up. Both the baseboard and the bottom plate start absorbing water.

24 to 48 hours

Wall Swells

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Growth is able to get going on the paper that lines the hidden side.

Day 3 to 7

Drywall Breaks Down

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. The amount of removal and reconstruction grows considerably.

Our Process

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Find Every Wet Area

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Sort Save From Remove

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

Walls we are keeping have their baseboards removed so air can be pushed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Dry and Rebuild

Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs and remaining drywall read dry, typically 3 to 5 days. The rebuild follows with fresh insulation, drywall installation and finishing, trim and a coat of paint.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Electrical Hazards Low Down

Water pools at the same height as most receptacles and cable runs. Until that circuit is inspected, avoid plugging anything into the affected wall.

Growth Behind the Paint

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. That side may be coated in growth even as the room side appears spotless.

Insulation Holding Water

Batts of fiberglass absorb water and keep it pressed to the framing. A wall with wet insulation will not dry from the room side alone.

Moisture in Exterior Walls

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.
