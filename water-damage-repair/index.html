Maintenance Tips for Water Damage Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Laundry rooms, bathrooms and kitchens should be rebuilt with moisture resistant wallboard and proper backer board.
  • Completed-repair photos and the scope document belong in your home records.
  • Hold off on heavy traffic and wet cleaning until new adhesives, caulk and paint have cured.
  • Before a contractor closes a floor or wall, request the final moisture numbers.
  • A water stain should stay unpainted and unpatched while its cause is still active.
  • Rebuilt bathroom walls last longer when the exhaust fan runs through and after each shower.

How We Handle Water Damage Repair

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage repair from start to finish:

1

Verify Dryness First

We take final moisture readings on framing, subfloor and remaining drywall. Reconstruction only begins once those numbers are at normal levels for the material, so nothing damp gets sealed in.

2

Put the Scope in Writing

We hand you a list, room by room, covering what gets replaced, planned materials and the likely schedule. If a claim is open, we line the scope up with the adjuster.

3

We refill open cavities with insulation, hang, tape and finish drywall, and repair the subfloor. After that we install trim, cabinetry and floors, ordering the work so completed surfaces are not damaged.

4

Final Finishes and Walkthrough

The last stage covers texture matching, primer, paint and putting hardware, doors and baseboards back. Then we walk every room with you and fix anything on the list before we call the job complete.

Signs It Is Time for Water Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Baseboards and door casings are warped or no longer sit flat against the wall.
  • Hardwood boards have cupped, crowned or lifted and did not flatten after drying.
  • Tiles sound hollow, have come loose or show cracked grout since water got beneath them.
  • Flooring flexes more than it should in the spot that was flooded.
  • Since the water damage, certain doors drag on the jamb or miss the latch.
  • Laminate or vinyl plank edges are swollen, curled or separating at the seams.
  • A leak overhead left the ceiling drywall cracked, sagging or falling apart.
